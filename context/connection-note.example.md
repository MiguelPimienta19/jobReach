# Connection Note Format

Rules and one calibrated example for ≤280-character LinkedIn connection notes. Loaded only by the connection-note generation call. Replace the example below with one of your own real notes — the AI uses it to calibrate tone and structure.

---

## Rules

- ≤280 characters. LinkedIn caps at 300 with Premium, 200 without.
- Plain, direct, one specific reason for reaching out.
- Mention the specific company, role, or shared school/background.
- Vary at least one specific detail per recipient (their title, team, or something from their profile). Never send the exact same note to two people at the same company.
- No year/seniority words. Use the role title's framing instead.
- No "I hope this finds you well." No "I'd love to connect" as filler.
- "Would love to connect" is OK if paired with a specific ask.
- One intro pattern, used consistently. Pick one and stick to it.

---

## Pattern

1. **Greeting:** "Hi [FirstName],"
2. **Intro:** "I'm [Your Name], a [school + major / current role]."
3. **Why you're reaching out:** "Just applied for the [exact role title] at [Company]"
4. **Relationship hook (varies by contact type):**
   - University recruiter / recruiter: "saw you handle [team/region] hiring there"
   - Alum: "noticed you're a [school] alum on the team"
   - Engineer (referral): "came across your profile" / "saw you work on [their team]"
5. **Specific ask:** prefer concrete asks ("get my application in front of the right hiring team") over vague ones ("any advice you might have").

---

## Example

> Hi [FirstName], I'm [Your Name], a [school] [major] student. Just applied for the [role title] at [Company] and noticed you're [alum / on the team / handle hiring there]. Would love any advice on [specific concrete thing].

---

## What makes a good note

- One specific detail per recipient — their team, role, or something visible on their profile.
- "Just applied" beats "I just applied" — saves characters.
- "Would love" beats "I'd love to connect and hear" — tighter.
- The ask is concrete. "Get my application in front of the right hiring team" reads better than "any advice you might have."
