# Cover Letter Format

Rules and one calibrated example for generated cover letters. Loaded only by the cover-letter generation call. Replace the example below with one of your own real letters — the AI uses it to calibrate tone, sentence length, and structure.

---

## Rules

- Under 350 words
- Plain text. No headers, no bullets, no bold inside the body.
- Format (header block then body):
  - `[Today's date]`
  - `[Contact Name]` (skip if unknown)
  - `[Contact Title]` (skip if unknown)
  - `[Company Name]`
  - (blank line)
  - `Dear [Contact Name]:` — or `Dear Hiring Team:` if no name
  - (blank line)
  - P1: Who you are + why this company specifically
  - P2: 1–2 real projects with stack and outcome
  - P3: Why this role fits where you're going
  - P4 (optional): One human detail
  - (blank line)
  - `Sincerely,`
  - `[Your Name]`
- No street address or zip. Redundant with the application form.
- Open the body with something specific. Never throat-clearing.
- End with confidence, not desperation.

---

## Example: [Company] — [Role] ([Month Year])

Hiring Team at [Company],

[Sentence one: a sharp claim about the company or the role. No throat-clearing. Mention who you are in one short clause.]

[Paragraph two: 1–2 real projects. Name the company or event, the stack, and the outcome in plain language. "I built X at Y. It runs on Z. It does W."]

[Paragraph three: why this role fits where you're going. Be honest about what you'd be learning. State what you want.]

[Optional paragraph four: one human detail — a club, a side project, something real that ties to the company's posture.]

Thanks for your time and consideration.

[Your Name]

---

## What makes a good example

- Opener is specific to the company or role, not a generic greeting.
- Projects are named, stacks are named, outcomes are concrete.
- The "why this role" paragraph is honest about gaps without apologizing.
- Every sentence has a specific noun. No filler.
- Ends with confidence, not "I hope to hear back soon."
